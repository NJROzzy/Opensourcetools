    Amalgam

    by NJR

    A field guide to the free and open-source instruments with which one may build a proper project across Computer Science — from hardware, software and computation to networks, data, distributed systems and security — and in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Robotics and emerging computing: catalogued, annotated, and read against the currents of the day.

    Scroll
    § 1

    System Design Canvas

    Start with the graph. Load a ready-made system design for any domain, a path's common architecture or a recommended project, then make it yours: move boxes, connect components, add or swap open-source tools, leave notes, and export it as an image.

    § 2

    Build Paths

    Pick what you want to build. Each path shows the common architecture — the layers almost every project of that kind passes through, with the free, open-source tools for each layer — and projects you can open to see their full stack.

    § 3

    Architecture Studio

    Drag tools, or a whole project, onto the grid to assemble an architecture layer by layer, then switch to Graph to see how every layer and tool connects, and add or remove tools right there. The advisor points out empty layers, tools that pair well with yours, and the real projects your stack resembles.

    Drag onto the grid, or click to add. Drag a placed tool back here to remove it. On touch screens, press and hold to drag.

    § 4

    The Amalgam Map

    Where domains overlap, projects appear. Choose any three of the eleven domains, then view the map by project to see what can be built in each overlap, or by tool to see where every tool lives. Select a region or a logo for the full picture.

    § 5

    Recommendation Chains

    Choose a project and follow its chain: each link is a tool, the role it plays, and why the project needs it — in the order you would assemble them.

    § 6

    Currents & Trends

    The movements shaping open tooling right now, and the tools they touch.

      § 7

      Method

      On inclusion. A tool enters the Atlas only if it can be used at no cost and its source is published under a recognised open licence (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD, GPL, MPL and kin). For open-weight AI models, only releases under an open-source licence (Apache-2.0) are recommended. Nothing here requires a paid plan. Where a project moved to a source-available licence, the Atlas lists its open community fork instead.

      On assembly. A proper project is rarely one tool, or one domain — it is an amalgam. A robotics study might braid ROS 2, MuJoCo and PyTorch; an IoT monitor braids KiCad, Zephyr, MQTT and Grafana across hardware, networks and data. Every tool is tagged with each domain it serves, which is how the map places it. Read the Atlas as a set of parts, not a ranking.

      On currency. Licences change. Always verify the licence file in a project's repository before you build upon it.

      § 8

      The Atlas

      The full catalogue. Every entry is free to use and published under an open-source licence. Filter by any of the eleven domains, search by name, purpose, domain or licence, and open any card for details.
